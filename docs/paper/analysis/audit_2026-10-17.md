## (B) Prose claims the current numbers no longer support

1. **Abstract, 0_abstract.tex:24-25: "+6.8 accuracy over random selection at 5% on NExT-GQA".** The same claim appears at 6b:185-188 ("grows as the budget shrinks") and in the Limitations at 7_conclusion.tex:26-27 ("its advantage appears at tighter budgets (5% on NExT-GQA)").
   - The 5% Cover/Random rows still use the zero-token-collapse rule, and their fit reruns have not started.
   - At 10% the fix raised both selectors by about 2 mIoU and changed Cover − Random. Cover − Random at 5% may change too.
   - Also unmatched: Cover and Random budgets count timestamps, so at a given nominal % they keep about 3× fewer *visual* tokens than SemVID. The "Coverage vs SemVID 5%" comparison is therefore not budget-matched. At matched visual tokens (debug block, 269 q), Cover10-fit − SemVID 3.4% is **+13.0\*** Acc@QA.
2. **Limitations, 7_conclusion.tex:25-27: "question-driven selection gains little over random selection at 10–30%".** With the fix, Cover − Random at 10% is **+3.4\* / +3.2\*** Acc@QA (Qwen3 / TL-8B). TL-8B also gains mIoP +1.9\*. Only 30% is n.s. (+1.2). The "small at moderate budgets" framing in the abstract (line 23-24) is weaker than the data for NExT-GQA at 10%.
3. **New fact the NExT-GQA prose misses:** with the fix, **Coverage 10% is indistinguishable from unpruned on every metric, for both models.**
   - Qwen3: Acc@QA −0.6, Acc@GQA −0.4, mIoU −0.7, all n.s.
   - TL-8B: −0.7, −0.7, +0.2, all n.s.
   - Random 10% loses about 4 Acc@QA\*.
   - The current table (buggy rows) shows Cover10 2.8–3.0 Acc@GQA below unpruned, which understates Coverage.
4. **Abstract, 0_abstract.tex:20: "[BOCC] matches an oracle that sees only the gold evidence on grounded answers".**
   - Qwen3 holds for GQ@0.5 only (−1.5 n.s.). Accuracy is **−5.3\*** [−10.1, −0.4], and 6_results.tex:537 calls this "interval just reaching 0".
   - TL-8B is far below its own gold window + PCD: **−11.0\* mIoU, −9.7\* Acc, −8.8\* GQ@0.5** (session "new").
   - The claim must be restricted to Qwen3 GQ@0.5 or removed.
5. **6b_transfer.tex:150-151: "With TimeLens-8B, BOCC answers and grounds better than Qwen3-VL-8B with the gold window (GQ@0.5 17.5 vs 15.8)".**
   - This is a cross-model comparison against the *weaker* model's oracle. TL-8B's own gold window gives GQ 19.2 (Acc 28.2, mIoU 46.6), and with PCD 25.9 (Acc 36.2, mIoU 54.9).
   - Sessions also differ: TL-8B CoCo is 17.5 in fresh43 and 17.1 in "new"; the TL-8B gold rows are in "new".
6. **6_results.tex:533-535: "At the same budget, BOCC without any gold information beats perfect token selection".** This is Qwen3 only. TL-8B CoCo − oracle selector + PCD is **+0.5 [−2.2, +3.4] mIoU, +2.7 n.s. GQ** (Acc +4.8 [0.0, +9.3]).
7. **6_results.tex:539-540: "PCD ... does not change its answers ... more evidence raises accuracy, while PCD fixes the time values. The two levers are complementary."** This is Qwen3 only. On TL-8B, PCD on the gold window raises **Acc +8.0\* and GQ@0.5 +6.7\*** (and mIoU +8.3\*).
8. **Intro, 1_intro.tex:105-106: "PCD helps reasoning-style answers on all eight memories we test and on both models".** The same idea appears at 6b:231 and 6_results.tex:415-420 ("PCD helps only where the answer format lets the prior choose the times") and in the Limitations at 7_conclusion.tex:27-28.
   - S-EMBER is fine. But on NExT-GQA with the joint *timeline* (reasoning-style) format, **PCD hurts TimeLens-8B**:
     - mIoU −4.4\* (unpruned), −4.5\* (Cover10) and −1.5\* (Random10);
     - mIoP −5.7\*, −5.9\* and −4.0\*;
     - ungrounded replies rise from 37 to 112 (format failures; R17 §3.4).
   - Under the NExT-GQA official S-EMBER-style joint prompt (`ofc`), PCD also lowers Qwen3 grounding:
     - −1.5\* mIoU on unpruned and −2.5\* on Cover10-fit;
     - clock-time lines more than double (22 → 58 and 24 → 89).
   - "On both models" must be scoped to S-EMBER, and the rule "PCD helps where the format lets the prior choose" needs this counterexample.
9. **6b_transfer.tex:41, 4_method.tex:104-106 and 3_diagnosis.tex:88-89: "under the one-line prompt PCD has no effect".**
   - The one-line PCD runs used **no reserve**, while the timeline PCD runs used **reserve 64** (R15 §2e: "PCD columns are not matched on reserve"). `tab:prompt` and its caption do not disclose this.
   - Mitigation that can be cited: on S-EMBER, reserve 64 alone changes grounding by +0.1 [−0.0, +0.3] (p50, R17 §4.3).
10. **Intro, 1_intro.tex:102-103 and 6b:79-80: "at 897 tokens [BOLT + Coverage] exceeds a 1 fps input of 1,125 tokens by +8.8\* mIoU".** The intro's wording attributes the gain to Coverage. On the full QVH set (R17 T3):
    - Retrieve-64 + Cover − TL 1 fps is **+9.2\*** (Qwen3) and **+6.5\*** (TL-8B).
    - Retrieve-64 + **Random** gets most of it: **+7.0\*** and **+6.1\***.
    - Cover − Random under Retrieve is +2.2\* for Qwen3 at 64 frames and +0.6 n.s. at 128 frames. For TL-8B it is **+0.4 n.s. (64) and −0.0 (128)**.
    - The gain is mainly Retrieve + KV pruning at full resolution. The SigLIP cost is not counted.
11. **Abstract, 0_abstract.tex:14-16: "61.7 vs 60.5 and 67.3 vs 67.1 mIoU ... (+3.5 and +2.0)".** These are 513-query subset numbers, and the abstract does not say so. The full sets (1,541 q) give **59.7 vs 59.6 and 65.4 vs 65.7**, with Cover − Random **+1.6\* / +1.2\***. The claim itself is not wrong; the abstract simply leads with the larger subset effects.
12. **`tab:fullsweep` caption, 6b:89-90: "low-res ... at a matched budget (about 3.2k / 4.2k visual tokens)".** The 3.2k/4.2k figures **could not be verified from the sources.**
    - The TL-script caps (3,500 / 4,500) count video tokens only.
    - Our kept counts (3,348 / 4,652) include timestamps.
    - So the low-res input sees at least as many visual tokens as Cover. That favours the paper's claim, but the matching should be described correctly.
13. **Tables mix batch sizes and clusters without saying so.** The 10%-fit and 5% NExT-GQA rows (hermes-new, bs 8) are paired against old-cluster unpruned runs (bs 2). The cross-cluster drift is ΔmIoU +0.4 n.s. (R17 ‡). Section 6b:7-9 mentions batch size but not the cluster.
