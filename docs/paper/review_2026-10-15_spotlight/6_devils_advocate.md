### Strongest Counter-Argument

After the revision, every positive result in this paper depends on a choice the authors made, and its one result that does not is negative. The large gains (QVHighlights R@0.5 54.8 vs 28.6; S-EMBER +11 mIoU over the uncompressed model; PCD +7 to +13 mIoU) all hold under a "timeline" answer format that the authors designed. Sec. 6.2 now shows this format costs the uncompressed model about 30 mIoU (−30.0*) against the standard one-line prompt. Under the standard prompt, PCD does nothing (−0.1, −0.3, −0.5; and +0.4 with the direct prompt). That points to a simpler explanation than a "video-free temporal prior" built into the decoder: a chronological `Seen: t₁ seconds…` scaffold naturally begins at 0 s, writes its interval last and stops early. PCD repairs a problem the prompt created. No evidence in the paper separates "the model has a prior" from "this format induces one", because the prior is never measured under any other format. What remains is Coverage: SnapKV-style question-attention eviction plus an equal per-frame quota. It is applied after full prefill, so it saves neither time-to-first-token nor peak memory. Under the standard prompt it matches the uncompressed model and beats random KV eviction by +2.0 to +3.5 mIoU, on one 513-query QVHighlights subset. It is level with random on Charades-STA, NExT-GQA and the same subset under the timeline prompt (−2.8*). Its novelty over plain question top-k without a quota is never measured: that cell of tab:design is a TODO. The robust finding, that QA pruners fall below random selection, compares input-level pruners with KV-level random eviction after full prefill, so "lost timeline coverage" competes with "the KV-level method saw every token". The paper is now a modest pruning note plus a format-specific decoding fix. Neither is a spotlight result, and the coupling between them is shown only on S-EMBER.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **Format pathology instead of a decoder prior (C1).** A chronological "Seen:" list is a generation scaffold. Under it, early first entries and early stops are expected, and PCD acts as a format corrector. This one explanation fits three facts: PCD's inertness under single-interval prompts, the 30-mIoU format cost, and the fact that the instruction fix ("do not assume near the start") fails (the scaffold, not the belief, sets the first token).
2. **Context-length degradation under the timeline format.** On QVH-513 with the timeline prompt, Random 31% (33.4) beats All tokens (30.4), and Coverage 31% only ties All tokens. Fewer tokens may help this format regardless of which tokens are kept. If so, part of the main-table "beats uncompressed" rows is context shortening, not selection. Test: random KV eviction at 50/75% under the timeline prompt.
3. **Post-prefill contextualisation instead of coverage (M1).** Random KV eviction keeps entries that attended to the full video, while input-level pruners do not. This competes with the "holes in the timeline" explanation for Obs. 1 across pruning levels.
4. **Length calibration instead of evidence (already partly addressed).** On pooled S-EMBER, ActivityNet and Charades, CoCo ties length rescaling. Only QVHighlights (timeline) and S-EMBER-closed separate the two. Both separations are under the timeline format, which links back to (1).
5. **Dataset-copy effects.** The large QVHighlights/ActivityNet timeline gains are on low-resolution re-encodes (153/~84 tokens per pair). The TimeLens-Bench copies at protocol resolution show far smaller Coverage effects. Token-per-frame scarcity may be what makes question selection matter, which narrows the claim to low-token-per-frame regimes.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- **Deployment and systems readers:** they need to know whether the budget saves anything at all (latency, memory, energy), given full prefill per question. The paper has no cost table.
- **Users of standard grounding prompts and grounding-tuned models:** practitioners run one-line prompts (TimeLens, VTimeLLM, etc.). For them the value is "Coverage ≈ unpruned at 24% KV" with unclear compute savings. The paper should say this plainly.
- **Benchmark maintainers:** the floor contribution currently blames benchmarks for what is partly a prompt effect. Its framing affects how others read Charades-STA/ActivityNet results.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper assumes that a budget on the *KV cache read by the decoder* is the relevant budget for video LLMs. All its comparisons treat kept-token count as the cost axis. But Coverage needs every token encoded and prefilled with the actual question first. The paper never examines whether a method that has already processed everything, then discards most of it, belongs to the same problem as pruners that never process the dropped tokens.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The revision is unusually candid. Sec. 6.2 and the Limitations admit the prompt handicap, PCD's narrow scope and the short-video failures, and the negative-results table is real. That candour is the paper's strongest asset, and it is what exposes C1–C3. The front matter has simply not been updated to match.
- The reproduction checks (TimeLens script within 0.3 mIoU of published numbers on six cells; same-query bridges n.s.) are solid and should stay.
- The video-free floors and length-rescaling baselines are a real methodological contribution, whatever the prompt question turns out to be.
- The NExT-GQA "Coverage keeps answers (+3.1* Acc@QA on both models)" result is consistent across models. It may be a cleaner selling point for Coverage than grounding gains are.
- The S-EMBER TimeLens-8B result (+10.7*, same method ordering) is real evidence of transfer across models *within the format*. If C2 comes out favourably, it becomes the paper's spotlight claim.
