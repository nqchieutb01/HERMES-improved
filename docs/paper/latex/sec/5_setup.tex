\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
Our main model is Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct~\cite{qwen3vl}. It sees 64 frames at native resolution, sampled uniformly
from $[0,q]$ or chosen by BOLT (\cref{sec:retrieval}), and decodes greedily or with PCD, at batch size 8 for pruned
memories. On S-EMBER we generate at most 384 new tokens with repetition penalty 1.1. On TimeLens-Bench the limit is
512, and on NExT-GQA it is 256 for the two-query setting or 1{,}024 for the joint answer, both with repetition
penalty 1.0. Unless stated otherwise, all methods share the timeline prompt. In \cref{sec:transfer} we add the
one-line prompt of TimeLens-Bench and TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens}, a Qwen3-VL-8B model fine-tuned for grounding.
TimeLens-8B frames are capped at about 1.57\,MP on S-EMBER and NExT-GQA. On TimeLens-Bench both models use the
protocol input (2\,fps, at most 14{,}336 visual tokens and at least 64 per frame), and BOLT runs there use 64 or 128
frames of at most 0.79\,MP. Batch size alone changes about half of the greedy outputs and moves a score by up to
about two points. Comparisons with the uncompressed model, which runs at batch size 1--2, include this noise.

\paragraph{Two clusters.}
We made the later runs on a second GPU cluster. These include most fixed-rule and all 5\% NExT-GQA runs, BOLT on the
full QVHighlights set, the official S-EMBER prompt, and the TimeLens-8B gold-informed runs. Some paired differences
combine runs from both clusters (marked where they occur). Runs repeated on both clusters agree
within noise. NExT-GQA without pruning moves by $+0.4$ mIoU (n.s.), with 469 of 510 replies identical. On S-EMBER,
random tokens move by $+0.2$ and \method by $-0.9$ mIoU (both n.s.), and the TimeLens script on QVHighlights moves by
$-0.2$ (n.s.).

\paragraph{Datasets.}
\textbf{S-EMBER} grounded QA~\cite{sember}: 475 questions (191 duration, 184 counting, 100 location) on the first
300 videos, asked mid-stream, so the memory contains only the frames before the question.
\textbf{QVHighlights}~\cite{qvhighlights} val: 500 queries with a single relevant window, sampled with seed 0 from
the 1{,}020 single-window queries, on videos of about 150\,s.
\textbf{ActivityNet Captions}~\cite{activitynet} val\_1: 500 queries, one sentence per video, on videos of about
115\,s. \textbf{Charades-STA}~\cite{charadessta} test: 500 queries on videos of about 30\,s. For these three datasets
the question is \emph{``When does the following happen in the video: \textless query\textgreater?''} and the window is
the whole video.
\textbf{TimeLens-Bench}~\cite{timelens}: the re-annotated Charades-STA, QVHighlights and ActivityNet sets, evaluated
with their one-line prompt on the full sets and on a 513-query QVHighlights subset. \textbf{NExT-GQA}~\cite{nextgqa}
test: 1{,}005 questions on videos of about 40\,s. The answer and the interval are asked either in two separate
queries (multiple choice or open-ended) or, for open-ended answers, in one joint reply. Our QVHighlights and
ActivityNet copies are low-resolution re-encodes with 153 and about 84 visual tokens per frame pair, compared with 660
on S-EMBER, so we also report budgets in absolute tokens.

\paragraph{Budget.}
The default budget is 10\% of the visual tokens of the 64-frame memory, timestamps included (about
2{,}140 tokens on S-EMBER, about 520 on QVHighlights). We report ActivityNet at 30\% (400--900 tokens), because at
10\% its memory holds only 120--300 tokens (\cref{sec:budget}). Since timestamps count against the budget, a KV
selector at a nominal $\rho$ keeps fewer visual tokens than an input pruner at the same $\rho$; on NExT-GQA at 10\%
it keeps about 3.4\% of the visual tokens. A frame's timestamp is kept when the frame keeps a visual token. An
earlier version of this rule could let timestamps fill the budget of very small memories and leave no visual token.
The NExT-GQA 10\% runs use the fixed rule (\cref{sec:transfer}) and the other runs use the earlier one. The
difference can matter only where timestamps take a large share of the budget (\cref{sec:budget}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against all tokens (no pruning), random tokens, and four QA pruners: FastVID~\cite{fastvid},
VidCom\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{vidcom2}, FlashVID~\cite{flashvid} (vision-side stages) and SemVID~\cite{semvid}. The
pruners are re-implemented and checked to keep the same tokens as the authors' code on test inputs. Each
baseline is run with and without PCD. A global question top-$k$ without quotas is compared in
\cref{sec:res-coverage}.

\paragraph{Floor-adjusted evaluation.}
For every dataset we report three video-free or video-light references. (i) The \emph{best constant
interval} $[aq, bq]$ over a $20{\times}20$ grid, tuned on the test questions, gives an upper bound for any constant
rule. (ii) \emph{Length rescaling} stretches the no-PCD prediction about its start by a factor of 1.76, fitted once
on S-EMBER and applied unchanged to the other datasets. It tests whether a gain could come from longer intervals
alone. (iii) We split the questions into \emph{closed} ones, whose gold interval ends before $0.8q$, and the rest.
End-snapping on S-EMBER is discussed in \cref{sec:suppl-snap}.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics.}
We report mIoU and R@$\{0.3,0.5,0.7\}$. On S-EMBER we also report answer accuracy and GQ@0.5, judged by a local
Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 model with the official S-EMBER judge prompt. Judging was repeated in three sessions, whose verdicts
for the same run differ by up to about one accuracy point. We therefore never pair Acc.\ or GQ@0.5
across sessions, and each table uses a single session, named in its caption: session A for \cref{tab:qa}, B for
\cref{tab:tl8b,tab:oracle}, and C for \cref{tab:oracle-tl8b}. The early-phase tables listed in \cref{sec:results}
use their own earlier sessions and are compared only within themselves. The same Qwen3-VL-8B
\method run scores 21.7 accuracy and 13.3 GQ@0.5 in session A and 21.9 and 13.5 in session B. The TimeLens-8B
\method run scores 27.4 and 17.5 in B and 26.5 and 17.1 in C. Open-ended NExT-GQA answers are judged by the same
model with the Video-ChatGPT prompt~\cite{videochatgpt}, in separate batches that share a cache. On S-EMBER, answers
that give times as minutes:seconds are converted to seconds, for every method alike (\cref{sec:suppl-parse}). The
joint NExT-GQA answers are scored with the official S-EMBER parser, which counts such times as missing. We
use a paired bootstrap over questions with 2{,}000 resamples, and we mark with $^*$ the differences
whose 95\% interval excludes 0.
