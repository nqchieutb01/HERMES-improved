\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct~\cite{qwen3vl}, 64 frames sampled uniformly from $[0,q]$ (or by BOLT, \cref{sec:retrieval}) at native
resolution, greedy decoding or PCD, batch size 8 for pruned memories. On S-EMBER we generate at most 384 new tokens
with repetition penalty 1.1; on TimeLens-Bench at most 512, and on
NExT-GQA at most 256 (two-query) or 1{,}024 (joint answer), both with repetition penalty 1.0. Unless stated
otherwise all methods share the timeline prompt. In \cref{sec:transfer} we add TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens}, a
Qwen3-VL-8B model fine-tuned for grounding, and the one-line prompt of TimeLens-Bench. TimeLens-8B frames are capped
at about 1.57\,MP on S-EMBER and NExT-GQA; on TimeLens-Bench both models use the protocol input (2\,fps, at most
14{,}336 visual tokens and at least 64 per frame), and BOLT runs there use 64 or 128 frames of at most 0.79\,MP.
Batch size changes about half of the greedy outputs and up to about two points of a score; comparisons with the
uncompressed model, which runs at batch size 1--2, carry this noise.

\paragraph{Two clusters.}
Later runs (most fixed-rule and all 5\% NExT-GQA runs, BOLT on the full QVHighlights set, the official S-EMBER prompt, and
the TimeLens-8B gold-informed runs) were made on a second GPU cluster, and some paired differences combine runs from
the two clusters (marked where they occur). Repeated runs across the clusters agree within noise: NExT-GQA without
pruning $+0.4$ mIoU (n.s., 469 of 510 replies identical); S-EMBER random tokens $+0.2$ and \method $-0.9$ mIoU (both
n.s.); the TimeLens script on QVHighlights $-0.2$ (n.s.).

\paragraph{Datasets.}
\textbf{S-EMBER} grounded QA~\cite{sember}: 475 questions (191 duration, 184 counting, 100 location) on the first
300 videos, asked mid-stream; the memory contains only the frames before the question.
\textbf{QVHighlights}~\cite{qvhighlights} val: 500 queries with a single relevant window (a seed-0 sample of the
1{,}020 single-window queries), videos of about 150\,s.
\textbf{ActivityNet Captions}~\cite{activitynet} val\_1: 500 queries, one sentence per video, videos of about
115\,s. \textbf{Charades-STA}~\cite{charadessta} test: 500 queries, videos of about 30\,s. For the last three, the
question is \emph{``When does the following happen in the video: \textless query\textgreater?''} and the window is
the whole video.
\textbf{TimeLens-Bench}~\cite{timelens}: the re-annotated Charades-STA, QVHighlights and ActivityNet sets, evaluated
with their one-line prompt (full sets and a 513-query QVHighlights subset). \textbf{NExT-GQA}~\cite{nextgqa} test:
1{,}005 questions on about 40\,s videos, answer and interval asked in two separate queries (multiple choice or
open-ended) or, open-ended, in one joint reply. Our QVHighlights and ActivityNet copies are low-resolution re-encodes (153 and about 84 visual
tokens per frame pair, against 660 on S-EMBER), so we also report budgets in absolute tokens.

\paragraph{Budget.}
10\% of the visual tokens of the 64-frame memory, timestamps included (S-EMBER: about 2{,}140 tokens;
QVHighlights: about 520). ActivityNet is reported at 30\% (400--900 tokens), because at 10\% the memory holds only
120--300 tokens (\cref{sec:budget}). Because timestamps count inside the budget, a KV selector at a nominal
$\rho$ keeps fewer visual tokens than an input pruner at the same $\rho$ (on NExT-GQA at 10\%, about 3.4\% of the
visual tokens). A frame's timestamp is kept when the frame keeps a visual token. An earlier version of this rule
could let timestamps fill the budget of very small memories and leave no visual token; the NExT-GQA 10\% runs use
the fixed rule (\cref{sec:transfer}), and the other runs use the earlier one, which can matter only where timestamps
take a large share of the budget (\cref{sec:budget}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
All tokens (no pruning); random tokens; FastVID~\cite{fastvid}, VidCom\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{vidcom2},
FlashVID~\cite{flashvid} (vision-side stages) and SemVID~\cite{semvid}, re-implemented and checked to keep the
same tokens as the authors' code on test inputs; each with and without PCD.
A global question top-$k$ without quotas is compared in \cref{sec:res-coverage}.

\paragraph{Floor-adjusted evaluation.}
For every dataset we report three video-free or video-light references. (i) The \emph{best constant interval}
$[aq, bq]$ over a $20{\times}20$ grid, tuned on the test questions, which upper-bounds any constant rule.
(ii) \emph{Length rescaling}: the no-PCD prediction stretched about its start by a factor fitted once on
S-EMBER (1.76) and applied unchanged to the other datasets; it tests whether a gain could come from longer
intervals alone. (iii) The split into \emph{closed} questions (gold end $<0.8q$) and the rest. End-snapping on
S-EMBER is discussed in \cref{sec:suppl-snap}.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics.}
mIoU and R@$\{0.3,0.5,0.7\}$; on S-EMBER also answer accuracy and GQ@0.5, judged with the official S-EMBER judge
prompt by a local Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 model. Judging was repeated in three sessions, whose verdicts for the same run
differ by up to about one accuracy point; Acc.\ and GQ@0.5 are never paired across sessions, and each table uses one
session, named in its caption: session A for \cref{tab:qa}, B for \cref{tab:tl8b,tab:oracle}, and C for
\cref{tab:oracle-tl8b}. The early-phase tables listed in \cref{sec:results} use their own earlier sessions and are
compared only within themselves. The same Qwen3-VL-8B \method run scores 21.7 accuracy and 13.3 GQ@0.5 in session A and 21.9
and 13.5 in session B; the TimeLens-8B \method run 27.4 and 17.5 in B and 26.5 and 17.1 in C. Open-ended NExT-GQA
answers are judged by the same model with the Video-ChatGPT prompt~\cite{videochatgpt}, in separate batches that
share a cache. On S-EMBER, answers that state times as minutes:seconds are converted to seconds for every method
alike (\cref{sec:suppl-parse}); the joint NExT-GQA answers are scored with the official S-EMBER parser, which counts
them as missing. We use a paired bootstrap
over questions (2{,}000 resamples) and mark with $^*$ differences whose 95\% interval excludes 0.
