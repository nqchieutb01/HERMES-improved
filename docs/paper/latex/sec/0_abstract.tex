\begin{abstract}
Video LLMs are increasingly asked not only \emph{what} happened but \emph{when}. A grounded question such as
``how long did I use the paper cutter?'' needs both an answer and the time interval of its evidence. To keep long
videos tractable, the visual memory of these models is pruned to a small fraction of its tokens, and published pruners
lose little multiple-choice accuracy at 10\% of the tokens. We show that temporal grounding needs something different:
\emph{pruning for QA keeps what is salient, but grounding needs what is everywhere}. A video LLM reads time from
timestamps, and a timestamp survives only if some visual token of its frame survives. Pruners that select by saliency,
uniqueness or text similarity empty whole frames, delete their timestamps, and fall below random token selection on
grounding. When the answer lists observed moments before giving the interval, a video-free prior fills the gaps: asked
with no video at all, the model still starts its intervals at the beginning of the window. We propose \method, a
training-free method that selects frames by the question (BOLT) and adds two parts. \emph{Coverage} keeps, in every
layer, the tokens that the actual question attends to most, with an equal per-frame quota so that every time anchor
survives. In the settings we tested, a global top-$k$ on the same question attention does as well without the quota,
so the question score is what drives the gain. Prior-contrastive decoding (PCD) writes each time value against the
model's own no-video prediction. Because the two parts behave differently across answer formats, we study the format
directly. With the standard one-line grounding prompt on the full Charades-STA and QVHighlights sets of
TimeLens-Bench, Coverage at 31\% of the tokens stays within 0.3 mIoU of the uncompressed model, for both a general and
a grounding-tuned model of the same family. It beats random selection by \key{$+0.5$} to \key{$+1.6$} and a
lower-resolution input of the same budget by \key{$+2.1$} to \key{$+2.6$}, while PCD has no effect on a 513-query
subset. In reasoning-style grounded QA (S-EMBER), PCD is the part that matters. With 10\% of the tokens, \method
improves on the uncompressed model by \key{$+11.2$} and \key{$+10.7$} mIoU under the same timeline prompt,
and by \key{$+5.5$} under the benchmark's official direct-answer prompt, although an input
restricted to the gold evidence still scores higher in accuracy, and on every metric for the grounding-tuned model.
Asking for a timeline of observations instead of a single interval costs single-moment grounding about 30 mIoU, so the
answer format is a major variable when evaluating compressed memories. We compare every method with video-free floors
and report where the method helps less. On short, densely sampled videos Coverage gains little on grounding
(\key{$+0.5$} mIoU on Charades-STA; NExT-GQA), although on NExT-GQA it keeps the accuracy of the uncompressed model at
10\% of the tokens while random selection loses about four points. PCD helps only where the answer format lets the
prior choose the times, and it lowers the scored grounding where it disturbs that format.
\end{abstract}
