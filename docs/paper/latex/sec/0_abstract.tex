\begin{abstract}
Video LLMs are increasingly asked not only \emph{what} happened but \emph{when}: a grounded question such as
``how long did I use the paper cutter?'' needs an answer and the time interval of its evidence. To keep long
videos tractable, the visual memory of these models is pruned to a small fraction of its tokens, and published
pruners lose little multiple-choice accuracy at 10\% of the tokens. We show that temporal grounding behaves
differently. Four recent training-free pruners fall \emph{below random token selection} on grounding, because a
model reads time from the timestamps that survive in memory, and selection by saliency, uniqueness or text
similarity removes whole stretches of the timeline. Where the memory is silent, the decoder fills the gap with a
prior that does not depend on the video: asked with no video at all, the model still answers, starting intervals
early and ending them too soon. We propose \method, a training-free method with two parts. \emph{Coverage} gives
every frame an equal token quota and fills it with the tokens the actual question attends to, so that every time
anchor survives pruning. \emph{Contrast} writes each time value against the model's own no-video prediction, with
a strength that fades as the model grows confident, and reserves room for the final interval. Because grounding
benchmarks carry strong positional biases, we evaluate every method against video-free floors. With 10\% of the
visual tokens, \method raises R@0.5 on QVHighlights from 25.0 (random tokens) and 28.6 (all tokens) to
\key{54.8}, where the best video-free constant reaches 17.6, and from 24.6 to \key{40.8} on S-EMBER grounded QA. We also report
where it does not help: on Charades-STA and ActivityNet Captions the uncompressed model itself stays below the
video-free floor, and no pruning method, ours included, rises above it.
\end{abstract}
