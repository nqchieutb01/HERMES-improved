\begin{abstract}
Video LLMs are increasingly asked not only \emph{what} happened but \emph{when}: a grounded question such as
``how long did I use the paper cutter?'' needs an answer and the time interval of its evidence. To keep long videos
tractable, the visual memory of these models is pruned to a small fraction of its tokens, and published pruners lose
little multiple-choice accuracy at 10\% of the tokens. We show that temporal grounding needs something different:
\emph{pruning for QA keeps what is salient, but grounding needs what is everywhere}. A video LLM reads time from
timestamps that survive only together with visual tokens of their frame, so pruners that select by saliency,
uniqueness or text similarity empty frames, delete their timestamps, and fall \emph{below random token selection} on
grounding. When the answer lists observed moments before the interval, the gaps are filled by a video-free prior:
asked with no video at all, the model still starts its intervals at the beginning of the window. We propose
\method, a training-free method that selects frames by the question (BOLT) and adds two parts. \emph{Coverage} keeps,
in every layer, the tokens that the actual question attends to most, under an equal per-frame quota that guarantees
every time anchor survives; with the question's attention, a global top-$k$ without the quota does as well in the
settings we tested, so the question score is what drives the gain. \emph{Prior-contrastive decoding}
(PCD) writes each time value against the model's own no-video prediction. The two parts behave differently across
answer formats, which we study directly. Under the standard one-line grounding prompt, on the full Charades-STA and
QVHighlights sets of TimeLens-Bench, Coverage at 31\% of the tokens is within 0.3 mIoU of the uncompressed model on a
general and a grounding-tuned model of the same family, and beats random selection (\key{$+0.5$} to \key{$+1.6$}) and a
lower-resolution input of the same budget (\key{$+2.1$} to \key{$+2.6$}), while PCD has no effect (on a 513-query subset). In
reasoning-style grounded QA (S-EMBER), PCD is what matters: with 10\% of the tokens \method improves on the
uncompressed model by \key{$+11.2$} and \key{$+10.7$} mIoU under the same prompt, although an input restricted to the
gold evidence stays above it in accuracy, and on every metric for the grounding-tuned model. Asking for a timeline of observations
instead of a single interval costs single-moment grounding about 30 mIoU, so the answer format is a first-order
variable in evaluating compressed memories. We evaluate every method against video-free floors and report where the
method helps less: on short, densely sampled videos the gain of Coverage on grounding is small (Charades-STA
\key{$+0.5$} mIoU; NExT-GQA), although on NExT-GQA it keeps the accuracy of the uncompressed model at 10\% of the
tokens where random selection loses about four points; and PCD helps only where the answer format lets the prior
choose the times, and lowers the scored grounding where it disturbs that format.
\end{abstract}
