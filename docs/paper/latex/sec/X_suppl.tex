\section{Parsing times written as minutes:seconds}
\label{sec:suppl-parse}

On videos longer than a minute, the model sometimes writes times as minutes:seconds, \eg
\texttt{Time: [1:45, 2:07]}, which the official S-EMBER parser scores as missing. We convert \texttt{h:mm:ss} and
\texttt{m:ss} to seconds for every method alike. Method rankings are unchanged with the official parser. The main
numbers with the official parser are: QVHighlights, \method 44.6\,/\,50.2 (\vs 48.8\,/\,54.8); ActivityNet at 30\%,

\section{End-snapping on S-EMBER}
\label{sec:suppl-snap}

Setting every predicted end to the question time $q$ raises every S-EMBER method to 42--46 mIoU, more than the 39.3
of \method, because 67\% of the gold intervals end within the last fifth of the window. On closed intervals,
end-snapping \emph{hurts} every method (to 22--24 mIoU) while \method keeps 39.7; on QVHighlights it lowers \method
from 48.8 to 40.5. A decoder that decides whether an interval is still open at question time is a natural
extension. \TODO{open-interval decoding.}

\scriptsize\ttfamily

model=qwen3\_vl\_8b experiment=sember\_grounding\_uniform run.uniform\_num\_frames=64 \\
dataset.grounding\_prompt=timeline run.keep\_time\_tokens=surviving run.max\_new\_tokens=384 \\
run.batch\_size=8 run.prune\_score=core\_topk run.offline\_keep\_ratio=0.1 \\
run.contrastive\_mode=blind run.contrastive\_alpha=1.0 run.contrastive\_adaptive=true \\
run.contrastive\_scope=time run.answer\_reserve=64 \\

\end{center}
