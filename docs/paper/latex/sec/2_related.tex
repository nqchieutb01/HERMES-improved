\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Frame selection.}
BOLT~\cite{bolt} scores frames with a contrastive image--text model and samples them by inverse transform sampling.
AKS~\cite{aks}, MDP3~\cite{mdp3}, FOCUS~\cite{focus} and Q-Frame~\cite{qframe} trade relevance against coverage or
diversity. All are evaluated on multiple-choice QA. We adapt BOLT to grounding (\cref{sec:retrieval}) and find that it
adds little once the question chooses the tokens.

\paragraph{Visual token pruning.}
Query-agnostic methods prune by redundancy or saliency. FastVID~\cite{fastvid} keeps density peaks per segment,
VidCom\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{vidcom2} budgets frames by uniqueness, FlashVID~\cite{flashvid} uses [CLS] attention
and tree merging, and DyCoke~\cite{dycoke} and FrameFusion~\cite{framefusion} merge similar tokens. Query-aware methods
use the text. SemVID~\cite{semvid} combines word-embedding relevance with MMR diversity, AdaTP~\cite{adatp} boosts
text-like segments, LGTTP~\cite{lgttp} reallocates tokens by temporal words, and PruneVid~\cite{prunevid} keeps the
top 40\% of tokens by question attention at one layer. KTV~\cite{ktv}, the closest in structure, selects keyframes and
then tokens under a relevance-weighted per-frame budget. Only LGTTP evaluates grounding, at a milder budget and
offline. \method uses an equal per-frame quota to keep every timestamp, and scores tokens by the actual question's
attention propagated exactly through all layers. We show that emptying frames hurts grounding, and that with the
actual question's attention the quota is a safeguard and not where the gain comes from (\cref{sec:res-coverage}).

\paragraph{KV-cache compression and video memory.}
SnapKV~\cite{snapkv}, H\textsubscript{2}O~\cite{h2o}, PyramidKV~\cite{pyramidkv} and Ada-KV~\cite{adakv} evict KV
entries by attention in long-context LLMs. Our token stage is SnapKV-style eviction for video with a per-frame
constraint. For streaming video, HERMES~\cite{hermes} compresses the cache with generic pseudo-queries,
ReKV~\cite{rekv} retrieves KV blocks when the question arrives, and StreamMem~\cite{streammem} and
InfiniPot-V~\cite{infinipotv} keep bounded memories. All report QA only. We build a memory per question from the
frames seen up to the question time. It is causal but not a persistent streaming memory, and we make no claim about
its build cost (\cref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Contrastive decoding and prior correction.}
Contrastive Decoding~\cite{contrastivedecoding} subtracts a weaker ``amateur'' model and introduced the plausibility
constraint we use. Classifier-free guidance~\cite{cfglm} and context-aware decoding~\cite{cad} amplify the difference
between predictions with and without the context, the form of our \cref{eq:pcd}. Correcting for a prior measured with
a content-free input is older. Contextual calibration~\cite{calibratebeforeuse} rescales label probabilities by the
answer to ``N/A'', and domain-conditional PMI~\cite{dcpmi} reweighs answers by their task-conditioned prior. In
vision-language models, VCD~\cite{vcd}, ICD~\cite{icd} and M3ID~\cite{m3id} contrast against a noised image, a
disturbed instruction and no image, PAI~\cite{pai} amplifies image attention and subtracts text-only logits, and
SEASON~\cite{season} uses temporally corrupted video negatives. These target hallucination, faithfulness or
classification. PCD applies the contrast only to \emph{time values}, with a confidence-adaptive strength, and we study
when it helps and when it does not (\cref{sec:res-contrast,sec:transfer}).

\paragraph{Grounding-tuned video LLMs.}
TimeChat~\cite{timechat}, VTimeLLM~\cite{vtimellm} and LITA~\cite{lita} train video LLMs to localise moments with
timestamp-aware encoders, boundary-aware training or time tokens. TimeLens~\cite{timelens} re-annotates the standard
benchmarks and fine-tunes Qwen-VL with reinforcement learning. All use a full visual memory. We use TimeLens-8B and
TimeLens-Bench and ask how such a model fares when its memory is pruned.

\paragraph{Training-free temporal grounding.}
NumPro~\cite{numpro} draws frame numbers on frames, FV-Action~\cite{fvaction} scans the video with yes/no questions,
REZE~\cite{reze} recasts grounding as recognition, and \citet{knowwhen} read intervals from calibrated attention heads,
debiased against a blank video. All assume the full set of video tokens. \method grounds from a \emph{compressed}
memory, and these readouts could run on top of it \TODO{run FV-Action and the attention readout on the same 10\% memory}.

\paragraph{Benchmark biases.}
\citet{otani2020uncovering} showed that blind baselines are competitive on Charades-STA and ActivityNet Captions
because the annotations carry positional priors.
