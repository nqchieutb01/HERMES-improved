\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Frame selection.}
BOLT~\cite{bolt} scores frames against the query with a contrastive image--text model and then picks them by inverse
transform sampling. AKS~\cite{aks}, MDP3~\cite{mdp3}, FOCUS~\cite{focus} and Q-Frame~\cite{qframe} optimise
relevance jointly with coverage or diversity. All of them are evaluated on multiple-choice QA. Our frame selection
front end (\cref{sec:retrieval}) adapts BOLT to grounding, and we find that it adds little once the tokens are chosen
by the question.

\paragraph{Visual token pruning for video LLMs.}
Query-agnostic methods compress the video by redundancy or saliency. FastVID~\cite{fastvid} segments the video and
keeps density peaks, VidCom\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{vidcom2} sets each frame's budget by how unique the frame is,
and FlashVID~\cite{flashvid} selects tokens by [CLS] attention and merges them across frames with trees.
DyCoke~\cite{dycoke} and FrameFusion~\cite{framefusion} merge similar tokens. Query-aware methods also use the text.
SemVID~\cite{semvid} scores tokens by their word-embedding relevance with MMR diversity, AdaTP~\cite{adatp} boosts
segments that resemble the text, LGTTP~\cite{lgttp} reallocates tokens according to temporal words in the question,
and PruneVid~\cite{prunevid} keeps the top 40\% of tokens by question attention at a single LLM layer.
KTV~\cite{ktv} is the closest in structure, since it selects keyframes first and then tokens under a
relevance-weighted per-frame budget. LGTTP is the only method we know of that evaluates grounding, and it does so at a
milder budget and offline. \method differs from these methods in three ways. Its per-frame quota is equal by design,
its score is the attention of the actual question propagated exactly through all layers, and the quota is there to
keep every timestamp. We show that emptying frames hurts grounding. We also show that once the actual question's
attention is used, the quota acts as a safeguard and is not where the gain comes from (\cref{sec:res-coverage}).

\paragraph{KV-cache compression and video memory.}
In long-context LLMs, SnapKV~\cite{snapkv}, H\textsubscript{2}O~\cite{h2o}, PyramidKV~\cite{pyramidkv} and
Ada-KV~\cite{adakv} evict KV entries by attention. Our token stage is SnapKV-style eviction applied to video, with a
per-frame constraint added. For streaming video, HERMES~\cite{hermes} compresses the cache with generic
pseudo-queries, ReKV~\cite{rekv} retrieves KV blocks when the question arrives, and StreamMem~\cite{streammem} and
InfiniPot-V~\cite{infinipotv} maintain bounded memories. These works report QA only. We build our memory for each
question from the frames seen up to the question time. It is therefore causal, but it is not a persistent streaming
memory, and we make no claim about the cost of building it (\cref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Contrastive decoding and prior correction.}
Contrasting a model's prediction with that of a weaker or less informed model is an established decoding tool.
Contrastive Decoding~\cite{contrastivedecoding} subtracts a small ``amateur'' model and introduced the plausibility
constraint that we use. Classifier-free guidance for language models~\cite{cfglm} and context-aware
decoding~\cite{cad} amplify the difference between the predictions made with and without the conditioning context,
which is the form of our \cref{eq:pcd}. The idea of measuring the prior with a content-free input and correcting for
it is older still. Contextual calibration~\cite{calibratebeforeuse} rescales label probabilities using the model's
answer to ``N/A'', and domain-conditional PMI~\cite{dcpmi} reweighs answers by their task-conditioned prior. For
vision-language models, VCD~\cite{vcd} contrasts against a noised image, ICD~\cite{icd} against a disturbed
instruction and M3ID~\cite{m3id} against the absence of the image, while PAI~\cite{pai} amplifies image attention and
subtracts text-only logits. SEASON~\cite{season} carries the idea over to video with temporally corrupted negatives.
These methods target hallucination, faithfulness or classification. PCD uses the same contrast for another purpose.
We apply it only to \emph{time values}, with a strength that adapts to the model's confidence, and we study when it
helps and when it does not (\cref{sec:res-contrast,sec:transfer}).

\paragraph{Grounding-tuned video LLMs.}
TimeChat~\cite{timechat}, VTimeLLM~\cite{vtimellm} and LITA~\cite{lita} train video LLMs to localise moments using
timestamp-aware encoders, boundary-aware training stages or dedicated time tokens. TimeLens~\cite{timelens}
re-annotates the standard grounding benchmarks and fine-tunes Qwen-VL models with reinforcement learning. All of these
works improve grounding with a full visual memory. We use TimeLens-8B as our grounding-tuned model and TimeLens-Bench
as the standard protocol, and we ask what happens to such a model when its memory is pruned.

\paragraph{Training-free temporal grounding.}
NumPro~\cite{numpro} draws frame numbers onto the frames, FV-Action~\cite{fvaction} scans the video with yes/no
questions, and REZE~\cite{reze} recasts grounding as recognition. \citet{knowwhen} read intervals from calibrated
attention heads and debias them against a run on a blank video. All of these methods assume the full set of video
tokens. \method addresses the complementary problem of grounding from a \emph{compressed} memory, and these readouts
