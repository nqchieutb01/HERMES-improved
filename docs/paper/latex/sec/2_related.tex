\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Frame selection.}
BOLT~\cite{bolt} scores frames against the query with a contrastive image--text model and selects them by inverse
transform sampling; AKS~\cite{aks}, MDP3~\cite{mdp3}, FOCUS~\cite{focus} and Q-Frame~\cite{qframe} optimise
relevance together with coverage or diversity. All are evaluated on multiple-choice QA. Our optional retrieval
front end (\cref{sec:retrieval}) is BOLT adapted to grounding, and we find that it contributes little once tokens
are chosen by the question.

\paragraph{Visual token pruning for video LLMs.}
Query-agnostic methods compress by redundancy or saliency: FastVID~\cite{fastvid} segments the video and keeps
density peaks, VidCom\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{vidcom2} gives each frame a budget by its uniqueness,
FlashVID~\cite{flashvid} selects by [CLS] attention and merges tokens across frames with trees, and
DyCoke~\cite{dycoke} and FrameFusion~\cite{framefusion} merge similar tokens. Query-aware methods use the text:
SemVID~\cite{semvid} scores tokens by word-embedding relevance with MMR diversity, AdaTP~\cite{adatp} boosts
segments similar to the text, LGTTP~\cite{lgttp} reads temporal words in the question to reallocate tokens, and
PruneVid~\cite{prunevid} keeps the top 40\% of tokens by question attention at one LLM layer. KTV~\cite{ktv} is
closest in structure (keyframes, then tokens with a relevance-weighted per-frame budget), and LGTTP is the only
method we know that evaluates grounding, at a milder budget and offline. \method differs in three ways: the
per-frame quota is equal by design, the score is the actual question's attention propagated exactly through all
layers, and the quota exists to keep every timestamp, which we show matters for grounding.

\paragraph{KV-cache compression and video memory.}
SnapKV~\cite{snapkv}, H\textsubscript{2}O~\cite{h2o}, PyramidKV~\cite{pyramidkv} and Ada-KV~\cite{adakv} evict
KV entries by attention in long-context LLMs; our token stage is SnapKV-style eviction applied to video with a
per-frame constraint. For streaming video, HERMES~\cite{hermes} compresses the cache with generic pseudo-queries,
ReKV~\cite{rekv} retrieves KV blocks at question time, and StreamMem~\cite{streammem} and
InfiniPot-V~\cite{infinipotv} maintain bounded memories. These works report QA only. Our memory is built per
question from the frames up to the question time: it is causal, but it is not a persistent streaming memory, and
we make no claim about the cost of building it (\cref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Contrastive decoding.}
VCD~\cite{vcd} contrasts the output distribution against a noised image, ICD~\cite{icd} against a disturbed
instruction, M3ID~\cite{m3id} against the absence of the image, and PAI~\cite{pai} amplifies image attention
while subtracting text-only logits; SEASON~\cite{season} extends the idea to video with temporally corrupted
negatives. These methods target object hallucination or QA. We apply the no-input contrast to \emph{time values
only}, with a confidence-adaptive strength, and test whether its benefit for grounding is more than a uniform
lengthening of the interval (\cref{sec:lengthening}).

\paragraph{Training-free temporal grounding.}
NumPro~\cite{numpro} draws frame numbers on the frames, FV-Action~\cite{fvaction} scans the video with yes/no
questions, REZE~\cite{reze} recasts grounding as recognition, and \citet{knowwhen} read intervals from calibrated
attention heads, debiased against a blank-video run. All assume the full set of video tokens. \method addresses the

\paragraph{Benchmark biases.}
\citet{otani2020uncovering} showed that blind baselines are competitive on Charades-STA and ActivityNet Captions
because of positional priors in the annotations. We find the same on the datasets we use, and make video-free
floors part of every comparison.
