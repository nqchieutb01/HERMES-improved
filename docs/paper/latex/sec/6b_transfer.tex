%% ================================================================================================= 6.2 transfer
\subsection{Standard prompt, a grounding-tuned model, and short videos}
\label{sec:transfer}

The results above use one model and the reasoning-style timeline prompt on every dataset. We now test the method
under the standard one-line grounding prompt of TimeLens-Bench~\cite{timelens}, on a grounding-tuned model
(TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens}), and on NExT-GQA~\cite{nextgqa}. Unless stated otherwise, paired differences use the
same questions; unpruned runs use batch size 1--2 and pruned runs batch size 8, which alone moves scores by up to
about two points (\cref{sec:setup}). Some runs were made on a second GPU cluster; where a paired difference combines
runs from the two clusters we say so, and repeated runs across clusters differ by less than one mIoU point, none
significantly (\cref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph{Our pipeline reproduces published numbers.}
With the TimeLens evaluation script, Qwen3-VL-8B scores 48.5, 59.6 and 47.1 mIoU on the full Charades-STA, QVHighlights
and ActivityNet sets of TimeLens-Bench (published: 48.3, 59.4, 46.8), and TimeLens-8B 55.4, 65.7 and 53.2 (published:
55.2, 65.5, 53.2). On the same queries, our own pipeline without pruning matches the script: $+0.5$ (Charades, 400
queries), $+1.4$ (ActivityNet, 400) and $0.0$ (QVHighlights, 513) mIoU, none significant (\cref{tab:anchors}).

\paragraph{The timeline prompt is costly for single-moment grounding.}
\Cref{tab:prompt} changes only the prompt. On QVHighlights queries, asking for a timeline of observations before the
interval costs about 30 mIoU against the one-line prompt ($-30.0^*$ without pruning, $-31.0^*$ for Coverage). PCD
recovers part of the loss ($+13.2^*$ on Coverage, $+10.4^*$ on random tokens) but stays far below the one-line prompt
($-17.8^*$), and under the one-line prompt it has no effect ($-0.1$, n.s.; also $-0.3$ and $-0.5$ with BOLT). These
one-line PCD runs used no answer reserve, unlike the timeline runs; the reserve alone does not change grounding on
S-EMBER ($+0.1$, n.s.), so we do not expect it to explain the difference, but the one-line comparison is not matched
on it. \TODO{one-line PCD with the 64-token reserve.} With
the timeline prompt, Coverage is not better than random selection on these queries ($-2.8^*$ greedy, $+0.1$ with PCD).
Two consequences follow. First, the QVHighlights, ActivityNet and Charades-STA numbers of \cref{tab:main} are
relative to a format that handicaps the uncompressed model; the gain over ``all tokens'' there (\cref{sec:main})
does not hold against the one-line prompt. Second, PCD corrects time values in reasoning-style answers, where the
model writes several times before the interval; when the answer is a single interval, there is little for it to
correct.

\paragraph{Under the standard prompt, Coverage transfers to both models.}
With the one-line prompt (\cref{tab:standard}, top), Coverage at 24\% of the visual tokens beats random selection on
both models ($+3.5^*$ and $+2.0^*$ mIoU), beats SemVID ($+2.9^*$), and beats the TimeLens input re-sampled to a
similar budget ($+3.7^*$, $+3.0^*$). It matches the uncompressed model ($+1.2$ for Qwen3-VL-8B, with a lower bound
just at zero; $+0.2$ for TimeLens-8B).

\paragraph{With BOLT frames, most of the gain over a low-resolution input comes from the front end.}
On the full QVHighlights set (\cref{tab:standard}, bottom), at about 1.2k tokens per query, BOLT with Coverage
exceeds a 1\,fps TimeLens input capped at 1{,}365 video tokens by $+9.2^*$ (Qwen3-VL-8B) and $+6.5^*$ mIoU
(TimeLens-8B), but BOLT with \emph{random} tokens already gains $+7.0^*$ and $+6.1^*$. Coverage adds $+2.2^*$ over
random tokens for Qwen3-VL-8B and $+0.4$ (n.s.) for TimeLens-8B; at 128 frames (about 4.5k tokens) it adds $+0.6$ and
$-0.0$ (n.s.). Most of this gain therefore comes from choosing the frames by the question and keeping their
resolution, not from Coverage, and the cost of the SigLIP index is not counted. (On the 513-query subset at 897
visual tokens, the corresponding numbers were $+8.8^*$ and $+6.8^*$ for BOLT $+$ Coverage over a 1{,}125-token
1\,fps input, and $+2.8^*$ for Coverage over random tokens with Qwen3-VL-8B.) At about 1.2k tokens both models lose
accuracy against their uncompressed memory (BOLT $+$ Coverage: $-2.5^*$ and $-2.1^*$ mIoU); at about 4.5k tokens
both are level with it ($+0.9$ and $-0.4$, n.s.) and above the 2\,fps low-resolution input capped at 4{,}500 tokens
($+3.2^*$ and $+2.0^*$). With the protocol input instead of BOLT frames, a budget of 8.2\% is filled by about 1.2k timestamp
tokens, no visual token survives, and both selectors collapse (4.6 and 4.2 mIoU): at small
budgets, fewer frames are needed (\cref{sec:frames}).

\paragraph{On grounded QA, the full method transfers to a grounding-tuned model.}
S-EMBER answers explain before they ground, so the timeline format is natural there. TimeLens-8B, trained for
grounding, is above Qwen3-VL-8B in every row of \cref{tab:tl8b}, and the ordering of the methods is the same for both
models. With 10\% of the tokens, \method improves on the uncompressed TimeLens-8B on every metric: $+10.7^*$ mIoU,
$+6.7^*$ accuracy and $+5.3^*$ GQ@0.5, and on random selection by $+15.7^*$ mIoU, $+7.8^*$ accuracy and $+8.0^*$
GQ@0.5. Each component helps on its own: PCD adds $+10.2^*$ mIoU to random tokens and $+6.7^*$ to BOLT $+$ Coverage,
and BOLT $+$ Coverage adds $+9.0^*$ to random tokens. A second judging session (C), which also scores the
gold-informed TimeLens-8B runs of \cref{sec:oracles}, gives 26.5 accuracy and 17.1 GQ@0.5 for \method (session B: 27.4
and 17.5); the two sessions agree within 1.1 accuracy points on every TimeLens-8B row, and every difference above
keeps its sign and significance ($+6.7^*$ accuracy and $+5.5^*$ GQ@0.5 over all tokens in session C). Within session
C, where Qwen3-VL-8B was rerun on the second cluster, \method on TimeLens-8B is above \method on Qwen3-VL-8B by
$+5.4^*$ mIoU, $+7.0^*$ accuracy and $+5.5^*$ GQ@0.5, whereas with random tokens the two models differ by only $+2.4$
mIoU (n.s.): the grounding-tuned model profits more from the method. TimeLens-8B's own gold-informed upper bounds,
however, remain well above \method (\cref{sec:oracles}).

\paragraph{On short videos, Coverage keeps the answers that random selection loses.}
NExT-GQA videos last about 40\,s, so 64 frames already sample them densely (\cref{tab:nextgqa}). At 10\%, Coverage
is indistinguishable from the uncompressed model on every metric for both models (Qwen3-VL-8B: $-0.6$ Acc@QA, $-0.4$
Acc@GQA, $-0.7$ mIoU; TimeLens-8B: $-0.7$, $-0.7$, $+0.2$; all n.s.), whereas random selection loses about four
points of accuracy ($-4.0^*$ and $-3.9^*$; these pairs combine the two clusters). Coverage therefore beats random
selection by $+3.4^*$ (Qwen3-VL-8B) and $+3.2^*$ Acc@QA (TimeLens-8B). On grounding the differences are small:
$+0.6$ mIoU (n.s.) for Qwen3-VL-8B, and $+1.9^*$ mIoP and $+1.1$ mIoU (n.s.) for TimeLens-8B, whose $+2.2$ Acc@GQA has
an interval reaching 0. At 30\% Coverage is lossless ($+0.5$ Acc@GQA, $-0.2$ mIoU, n.s.), is $+1.2$ Acc@QA above random
selection (n.s.), and grounds better than SemVID ($+1.8^*$ mIoU). When frames are dense, where tokens are kept
matters most for the answer and less for the interval, as on Charades-STA (\cref{sec:frames}).

The 5\% rows still use the earlier timestamp rule. With it, Coverage leads random selection by $+6.8^*$ (Qwen3-VL-8B)
and $+5.6^*$ Acc@QA (TimeLens-8B), but at 10\% the fixed rule raised both selectors by about 2.3 mIoU$^*$ and 2.4
Acc@GQA$^*$, so we do not interpret the 5\% differences until the reruns finish. SemVID at a nominal 5\% is not
budget-matched to Coverage at 5\%, because SemVID keeps about three times as many visual tokens.

\paragraph{Checks and a matched-budget comparison.}
\Cref{tab:nextgqa-debug} gives the floors of NExT-GQA on a subset. Without video, accuracy falls by $32.3^*$ points
(the options alone still give half of the answers) and grounding to 7.2 mIoU. Shuffling the frames leaves accuracy
unchanged and lowers mIoU by $6.7^*$; shuffling only the timestamps leaves accuracy unchanged ($+0.4$) and lowers
Acc@GQA by $12.6^*$ and mIoU by $10.9^*$: the interval is read from the timestamps, the answer is not. Keeping 100\%
of the tokens through either selector reproduces all 538 unpruned replies exactly, and batch size 1 instead of 8
leaves 502 of 538 replies unchanged and no metric significantly different. At the same number of visual tokens,
Coverage leads SemVID by $+13.0^*$ Acc@QA, $+9.3^*$ Acc@GQA, $+7.7^*$ mIoP and $+8.6^*$ mIoU at 3.4\%, and by
$+4.1^*$, $+5.2^*$, $+3.6^*$ and $+3.1^*$ at 24.9\% (Coverage 30\%: the full run restricted to these questions).
The fixed timestamp rule matters only for low-budget questions: on the 80 questions whose 10\% budget is about 281
tokens, the earlier rule left Coverage at 2.4 mIoU, with 85\% of its intervals starting at 0\,s; the fixed rule gives
32.4.

\paragraph{Open-ended answers.}
Without the options (\cref{tab:nextgqa-open}), answering is far harder (33.5 \vs 79.7 Acc@QA) while grounding is
unchanged, since the grounding query is the same. Our judge accepts the ground-truth answer for all 1{,}005 questions
and agrees with letter correctness on 99.2\% of the multiple-choice answers. Without pruning, Qwen3-VL-8B reaches 17.4
Acc@GQA zero-shot, against 21.4 for TOGA~\cite{toga}, which is trained for open-ended grounded QA and judged with
GPT-3.5. Coverage at 10\% keeps the answers in both protocols ($-0.3$ and $-0.9$ Acc@QA against all tokens, n.s.; cross-cluster pairs).
In the two-query protocol random selection is close as well (Coverage $+1.0$ Acc@QA and Acc@GQA, n.s.), and only
SemVID is clearly worse (Coverage leads it by $+4.2^*$ Acc@QA, $+4.0^*$ Acc@GQA and $+6.2^*$ mIoU). In the joint
timeline protocol random selection loses the answers ($-4.4^*$ against all tokens): Coverage leads it by $+3.5^*$
Acc@QA and $+1.5^*$ mIoU, and leads SemVID by $+5.2^*$ Acc@QA and $+3.2^*$ Acc@GQA. The joint timeline answer grounds
far worse than the separate query (20.8 \vs 37.7 mIoU): its listed moments start where the subject is first visible,
so 81\% of its intervals start at 0\,s (ground truth: 19\%). In this format PCD raises mIoU ($+5.6^*$ on all tokens,
$+4.3^*$ on Coverage with the earlier rule) but not Acc@GQA ($-0.9$ and $+0.1$, n.s.). In the two-query protocol PCD
slightly lowers grounding ($-1.5^*$ mIoU on all tokens; $-1.2^*$ on Coverage with the earlier rule) and leaves
Acc@GQA unchanged ($-0.5$, $-0.4$, n.s.), as with the one-line prompt on QVHighlights.

\paragraph{The official S-EMBER prompt on NExT-GQA.}
Asked with the official S-EMBER prompt, which requests the answer and the interval without a list of observations,
the uncompressed model answers and grounds better than with the timeline prompt ($+4.6^*$ Acc@QA, $+9.5^*$ mIoU).
Coverage and random selection at 10\% are both level with all tokens under this prompt ($+0.3$ and $-0.6$ mIoU,
n.s.). PCD raises accuracy slightly on all tokens ($+1.3^*$) but lowers mIoU ($-1.5^*$ on all tokens, $-2.5^*$ on
Coverage). Most of this loss is a format effect: PCD more than doubles the answers that write times as
minutes:seconds (22 $\rightarrow$ 58 on all tokens, 24 $\rightarrow$ 89 on Coverage), which the official parser
scores as missing. In every joint run, 67--81\% of the intervals still start at 0\,s.

\paragraph{TimeLens-8B, open-ended.}
For TimeLens-8B we have grounding only (answers not yet judged; 10\% runs with the earlier timestamp rule). In the joint
timeline format it grounds far better than Qwen3-VL-8B (36.3 \vs 20.8 mIoU, $+15.5^*$), and only 22--30\% of its
intervals start at 0\,s (Qwen3-VL-8B: 59--81\%). Here PCD \emph{lowers} grounding: $-4.4^*$ mIoU on all tokens,
$-4.5^*$ on Coverage and $-1.5^*$ on random tokens. The replies show why: TimeLens-8B often ends with an answer and no
\texttt{Time:} line, and PCD adds run-on or truncated replies, so replies without a parsable interval rise from 37 to
112 (scored as IoU 0). In the two-query protocol Coverage and random tokens at 10\% are level ($+0.1$ mIoU) and both
lose about 2 mIoU$^*$ against all tokens. \TODO{judge the TimeLens-8B open-ended answers; fixed-rule reruns of the
PCD 10\% rows.}

On NExT-GQA, the contribution is therefore question-driven token selection. PCD helps grounding only where the answer
format lets the prior choose the times (the joint timeline answer of Qwen3-VL-8B), and even there it does not raise
Acc@GQA; where it disturbs the answer format (TimeLens-8B, or minutes:seconds under the official prompt), it lowers
the scored grounding.
