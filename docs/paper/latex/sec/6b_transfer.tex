%% ================================================================================================= 6.2 transfer
\subsection{Standard prompt, a grounding-tuned model, and short videos}
\label{sec:transfer}

Apart from the one-line columns of \cref{tab:main}, the results so far use one model and the timeline prompt. Here
we test three further settings: the standard one-line prompt of TimeLens-Bench~\cite{timelens}, the grounding-tuned
TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens}, and the short videos of NExT-GQA~\cite{nextgqa}. Unless stated otherwise, paired
differences use the same questions. Unpruned runs use batch size 1--2 and pruned runs batch size 8, which alone can
move scores by up to about two points (\cref{sec:setup}). Some runs come from a second GPU cluster. Repeated runs
across the two clusters differ by less than one mIoU point, and we flag every pair that mixes them.

\paragraph{Our pipeline reproduces published numbers.}
With the TimeLens evaluation script, both models match the published scores on the full Charades-STA, QVHighlights
and ActivityNet sets of TimeLens-Bench (\eg 48.5 \vs 48.3 mIoU for Qwen3-VL-8B on Charades-STA). Without pruning, our
own pipeline agrees with the script on the same queries, within $0.0$ to $+1.4$ mIoU (\cref{tab:anchors}).

\paragraph{The timeline prompt is costly.}
\Cref{tab:prompt} changes only the prompt. On QVHighlights, asking for a timeline of observations before the
interval costs about 30 mIoU ($-30.0$ without pruning, $-31.0$ for Coverage). PCD recovers part of this loss
($+13.2$ on Coverage) but stays $-17.8$ below the one-line prompt, under which PCD has no effect ($-0.1$). Those
one-line PCD runs used no answer reserve, but on S-EMBER the reserve alone changes grounding by only $+0.1$.

The timeline-prompt results on QVHighlights, ActivityNet and Charades-STA (\cref{fig:teaser} and
\cref{tab:ladder,tab:budget,tab:frames}) therefore handicap the uncompressed model, and their gains over ``all
tokens'' do not hold against the one-line prompt, which the QVHighlights and Charades-STA columns of \cref{tab:main}
use. PCD corrects the times a reasoning-style answer writes before the interval, so a single-interval answer leaves
it little to correct.

\paragraph{Coverage under the standard prompt.}
With the one-line prompt (\cref{tab:standard}, top), Coverage at 24\% of the visual tokens beats random selection on
both models ($+3.5$ and $+2.0$ mIoU), and also SemVID and the TimeLens input at a similar budget. Against the
uncompressed model it scores $+1.2$ mIoU for Qwen3-VL-8B and $+0.2$ for TimeLens-8B.

\paragraph{BOLT frames on the full QVHighlights set.}
At about 1.2k tokens per query (\cref{tab:standard}, bottom), BOLT with Coverage exceeds a 1\,fps TimeLens input
capped at 1{,}365 video tokens by $+9.2$ mIoU on Qwen3-VL-8B and $+6.5$ on TimeLens-8B. Most of this gain comes from
choosing frames by the question at full resolution. BOLT with random tokens already gains $+7.0$ and $+6.1$, and
Coverage adds $+2.2$ and $+0.4$ over it. The comparison also leaves out the cost of the SigLIP index.

Against the uncompressed memory, BOLT $+$ Coverage loses grounding at about 1.2k tokens ($-2.5$ and $-2.1$ mIoU) and
is level at about 4.5k tokens ($+0.9$ and $-0.4$). With the protocol input instead of BOLT frames, about 1.2k
timestamp tokens use up a budget of 8.2\%, no visual token survives, and both selectors collapse (4.6 and 4.2
mIoU). At small budgets, fewer frames are needed (\cref{sec:frames}).

\paragraph{A grounding-tuned model on grounded QA.}
S-EMBER answers explain before they ground, so the timeline format is natural there. TimeLens-8B scores above
Qwen3-VL-8B in every row of \cref{tab:tl8b}, and both models rank the methods in the same order. With 10\% of the
tokens, \method improves on the uncompressed TimeLens-8B on every metric ($+10.7$ mIoU, $+6.7$ accuracy, $+5.3$
GQ@0.5) and leads random selection by $+15.7$ mIoU. Each component helps on its own. PCD adds $+10.2$ mIoU to random
tokens and $+6.7$ to BOLT $+$ Coverage, and BOLT $+$ Coverage adds $+9.0$ to random tokens.

A second judging session (C), which also scores the gold-informed runs of \cref{sec:oracles}, agrees with session B
within 1.1 accuracy points on every TimeLens-8B row, and every difference above keeps its sign. In it, with
Qwen3-VL-8B rerun on the second cluster, \method scores $+5.4$ mIoU higher on TimeLens-8B than on Qwen3-VL-8B, against
a gap of $+2.4$ with random tokens, so the grounding-tuned model profits more from the method. Its gold-informed upper bounds remain well above
\method.

\paragraph{Open-ended answers.}
Without the options (\cref{tab:nextgqa-open}), answering becomes far harder (33.5 \vs 79.7 Acc@QA), while grounding
is unchanged because the grounding query is the same. Our judge accepts the ground-truth answer for all 1{,}005
questions and agrees with letter correctness on 99.2\% of the multiple-choice answers. Without pruning, Qwen3-VL-8B
reaches 17.4 Acc@GQA zero-shot, against 21.4 for TOGA~\cite{toga}, which is trained for open-ended grounded QA and
judged with GPT-3.5. Coverage at 10\% keeps the answers in both protocols ($-0.3$ Acc@QA against all tokens in the
two-query protocol, a cross-cluster pair, and $+0.2$ with the official S-EMBER prompt). In the two-query protocol
random selection is close as well, and only SemVID is clearly worse (Coverage leads it by $+4.2$ Acc@QA and $+6.2$
mIoU). PCD there slightly lowers grounding ($-1.5$ mIoU on all tokens, and $-1.2$ on Coverage in a run with the
earlier timestamp rule) and barely changes Acc@GQA, as with the one-line prompt on QVHighlights.

\paragraph{Official S-EMBER prompt.}
This prompt asks for the answer and the interval in one reply, without a list of observations. Coverage and random
selection at 10\% are both level with all tokens ($+0.3$ and $-0.6$ mIoU). PCD raises accuracy slightly on all
tokens ($+1.3$) but lowers mIoU ($-1.5$ on all tokens, $-2.5$ on Coverage). Most of this loss is a format effect.
PCD more than doubles the answers that write times as minutes:seconds (22 to 58 on all tokens), which the
official parser scores as missing. In every run under this prompt, 68--76\% of the intervals
still start at 0\,s.

\paragraph{TimeLens-8B, open-ended.}
Here we have grounding results only. The answers are not yet judged, and the 10\% runs use the earlier timestamp
rule. In a joint answer with the timeline prompt, only 22--30\% of its intervals start at 0\,s. PCD lowers grounding
by $-4.4$ mIoU on all tokens, $-4.5$ on Coverage and $-1.5$ on random tokens. TimeLens-8B often ends with an answer
and no \texttt{Time:} line, and PCD adds run-on or truncated replies, so replies without a parsable interval (scored
as IoU 0) rise from 37 to 112. In the two-query protocol, Coverage and random tokens at 10\% are level ($+0.1$
mIoU), and both lose about 2 mIoU against all tokens. \TODO{judge the TimeLens-8B open-ended answers; fixed-rule
reruns of the PCD 10\% rows.}

On NExT-GQA, then, the method contributes through question-driven token selection. PCD does not raise Acc@GQA in
either protocol of \cref{tab:nextgqa-open}, and where it disturbs the answer format, as with TimeLens-8B or with
minutes:seconds under the official prompt, it lowers the scored grounding.
