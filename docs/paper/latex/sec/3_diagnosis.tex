\section{Grounding under a Token Budget: a Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

This section establishes Observations 1 and 2 on S-EMBER; Observation 3 (answer format) is studied in
\cref{sec:transfer}. The reference memory is random 10\% pruning unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

A question $x$ is asked at time $q$ about a video $v_{0:q}$. The model encodes $F{=}64$ frames from $[0,q]$ into a
KV cache $\Mem$ of $N$ visual tokens, interleaved with timestamp tokens (Qwen3-VL uses one ``\texttt{<t seconds>}''
per pair of frames). A budget $\rho$ (default $\rho{=}0.1$) keeps $\rho N$ tokens per layer, kept timestamps
included. The model answers with a short \emph{timeline}:
\begin{center}
\small\ttfamily
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\linewidth}\begin{tabular}{l}
Seen: $t_1$ seconds, \dots \\
Seen: $t_k$ seconds, \dots \\
Answer: \dots \\
Time: [$s$, $e$]
\end{tabular}\end{adjustbox}
\end{center}
and we parse $[s,e]$ from the last line. This format suits grounded QA, since the answer describes what was seen
before the interval. \Cref{sec:transfer} compares it with the one-line format of grounding benchmarks (\emph{``The event happens in $s$--$e$ seconds''}). We report mIoU and
R@$\{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$ against the gold interval, and GQ@0.5, the share of answers judged correct with IoU $\geq 0.5$.
An interval is an \emph{early start} if it starts in the first 5\% of the window, and a \emph{leak} if it starts
early although the gold evidence starts later.

\subsection{QA pruners lose temporal coverage}
\label{sec:coverage-loss}

At 10\% of the tokens, FastVID, VidCom\textsuperscript{2}, FlashVID and SemVID all score below random selection
(\cref{sec:pruners}). Where the kept tokens lie explains why (\cref{tab:retention}). HERMES scores by attention plus
recency and keeps 27.6\% of the last quarter of the window but 2.5--4.7\% elsewhere. FastVID keeps one representative
per run of similar frames, where a continuing event lives, and keeps tokens from only 25\% of the frames in a median
question. Random selection never empties a frame. A timestamp helps only if some content of its frame survives, so
emptied frames leave holes in the timeline even when the score is sensible. Ranking HERMES's scores within each frame (``stratified'', an equal share per frame) improves mIoU by
$5.3$.

\subsection{The prior fills the gaps}
\label{sec:prior}

\paragraph{The model answers without video.}
With an empty visual memory, the model still writes a timeline. 89\% of its first \texttt{Seen:} moments are at
0\,s and 90\% of its intervals start in the first 5\% of the window (an 88\% leak). The median interval is
0.45$\times$ the gold length, and 87\% of the ends fall more than half an interval too early. We call this habit the
\emph{temporal prior}.

\paragraph{The prior leaks into answers with video.}
The same signature appears, more weakly, in every memory (\cref{fig:leak}). About 30\% of intervals start early at
every random budget and without pruning, against 16\% of gold intervals. The share rises when coverage is lost, to
49\% for HERMES and 37--39\% for streaming HERMES. The leak follows where the memory is silent, not how many tokens
it keeps.

\paragraph{Removing the evidence causes the leak.}
We drop the uniform frames inside the gold interval (``evidence'') or as many frames elsewhere (``control''),
keeping every other frame with its true timestamp (\cref{tab:causal}). Without the evidence, answers move to the start
instead of spreading out, and the leak rises by 14 points for both the unpruned and the random 10\%
memory. In the HERMES 10\% memory, questions whose evidence keeps under 5\% of its tokens leak 56\% of the time,
against 33\% when at least 15\% survives.

\paragraph{An instruction does not remove it.}
Adding ``The relevant moments may occur at any point in the video; do not assume they are near the start'' to the
prompt barely moved the leak (29.5\%~$\rightarrow$~28.0\%) or grounding ($-0.1$ mIoU). In this format the prior acts
in the output distribution and must be removed there. We do not yet know whether it appears with single-interval
answers. There PCD has no effect (\cref{sec:transfer}; those runs omit the answer reserve, which alone changes S-EMBER
grounding by only $+0.1$), suggesting the prior decides fewer of the written times. \TODO{no-video answers and leak under the one-line and direct-answer prompts.}

\paragraph{The end is the weak boundary.}
Without contrastive decoding, the median end error across memories is 0.40--0.69 of the gold length and the
median start error 0.14--0.30. The most frequent failure, 33\% of answers, is an interval entirely \emph{before} the
gold one (\cref{tab:errors}).
