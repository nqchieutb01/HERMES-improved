\section{Grounding under a Token Budget: a Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

This section establishes Observations 1, 2 and 4 of the introduction on S-EMBER, before any method is introduced;
Observation 3, on the answer format, is studied in \cref{sec:transfer}.
Unless stated otherwise, numbers in this section use random 10\% pruning as the reference memory.

\subsection{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

A question $x$ is asked at time $q$ about a video $v_{0:q}$. The model sees $F{=}64$ frames from $[0,q]$, encoded
into a KV cache $\Mem$ of $N$ visual tokens interleaved with timestamp tokens (one ``\texttt{<t seconds>}'' per
pair of frames in Qwen3-VL). A budget $\rho$ (default $\rho{=}0.1$) limits the number of tokens kept in each layer
to $\rho N$, with kept timestamps counted inside the budget. The model answers with a short \emph{timeline}:
\begin{center}
\small\ttfamily
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\linewidth}\begin{tabular}{l}
Seen: $t_1$ seconds, \dots \\
Seen: $t_k$ seconds, \dots \\
Answer: \dots \\
Time: [$s$, $e$]
\end{tabular}\end{adjustbox}
\end{center}
from which the interval $[s,e]$ is parsed. This reasoning-style format is natural for grounded QA, where the answer
explains what was seen before it states the interval, and the whole diagnosis uses it. \Cref{sec:transfer} compares
it with the one-line format of grounding benchmarks (\emph{``The event happens in $s$--$e$ seconds''}). Grounding is scored by its IoU with the gold interval (mIoU and
R@$\{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$); grounded QA additionally requires the answer to be judged correct (GQ@0.5: correct answer
and IoU $\geq 0.5$). We call an answer an \emph{early start} when its interval starts in the first 5\% of the window,
and a \emph{leak} when it starts early although the gold evidence starts later.

\subsection{QA pruners lose temporal coverage}
\label{sec:coverage-loss}

At 10\% of the tokens, FastVID, VidCom\textsuperscript{2}, FlashVID and SemVID all score below random token
selection (\cref{sec:pruners}). The cause is visible in where the kept tokens lie (\cref{tab:retention}). HERMES's
attention-plus-recency score keeps 27.6\% of the tokens in the last quarter of the window and only 2.5--4.7\%
elsewhere; FastVID keeps a token from only 25\% of the sampled frames in a median question, because it retains one
representative per run of similar frames, and runs of similar frames are exactly where a continuing event lives.
Random selection keeps about 9\% everywhere and never empties a frame. A timestamp is useful only when visual content
of its frame survives with it, so a selector that empties frames leaves holes in the timeline. Holes hurt even when
the score itself is reasonable: ranking HERMES's scores within each frame (an equal per-frame share, ``stratified'')
instead of globally gains $5.3^*$ mIoU.

\subsection{The prior fills the gaps}
\label{sec:prior}

\paragraph{The model answers without video.}
Asked every question with an empty visual memory, the model still writes a timeline: 89\% of its first
\texttt{Seen:} moments are at 0\,s and 90\% of its intervals start in the first 5\% of the window (88\% leak). Its
intervals are 0.45$\times$ the gold length (median) and 87\% of its ends are more than half an interval too early. We
call this the model's \emph{temporal prior}: start early, stop soon.

\paragraph{The prior leaks into answers with video.}
The same signature appears in every memory, weaker (\cref{fig:leak}). About 30\% of intervals start early at every
random budget and with no pruning at all, against 16\% of gold intervals, and the share rises when coverage is lost
(HERMES 49\%, HERMES streaming 37--39\%). The leak is therefore not a matter of the number of tokens, but of where the
memory is silent.

\paragraph{Removing the evidence causes the leak.}
To test causality we drop the uniform frames that fall inside the gold interval (``evidence'') or the same number of
frames elsewhere (``control''), keeping every other frame with its true timestamp (\cref{tab:causal}). With the
evidence removed, the answers do not spread out: they move to the start of the video, and the leak rises by 14 points
for both the unpruned and the random 10\% memory. Within the HERMES 10\% memory, questions whose evidence keeps under
5\% of its tokens leak 56\% of the time, against 33\% when at least 15\% survives.

\paragraph{An instruction does not remove it.}
Adding ``The relevant moments may occur at any point in the video; do not assume they are near the start'' to the
prompt leaves the leak almost unchanged (29.5\%~$\rightarrow$~28.0\%) and grounding unchanged ($-0.1$ mIoU). Within this answer format, the
prior acts in the output distribution and has to be removed there. Whether the same prior appears when the model
answers with a single interval is an open question: there PCD has no effect (\cref{sec:transfer}), which suggests
that the prior decides fewer of the written times. \TODO{no-video answers and leak under the one-line and
direct-answer prompts.}

\paragraph{The end is the weak boundary.}
Across memories, the median end error is 0.40--0.69 of the gold length without contrastive decoding, against
0.14--0.30 for the start, and the most frequent failure is an interval entirely \emph{before} the gold one (33\% of
all answers, \cref{tab:errors}).

\subsection{Benchmark floors}
\label{sec:floors}

Positional regularities make some intervals predictable without the video (\cref{tab:floors}). On S-EMBER, questions
are asked while the stream is running and 67\% of gold intervals end within the last 20\% of the window, so the
constant $[0.15q, q]$ scores 36.9 mIoU. On ActivityNet Captions, whose moments cover 38\% of the video on average,
$[0, 0.95q]$ scores 37.9; on Charades-STA, $[0, 0.45q]$ scores 29.7. QVHighlights is the least biased (26.8 mIoU,
17.6 R@0.5). These constants are tuned on the test questions, so they upper-bound any video-free rule of this form. On
three of the four datasets the best constant beats the uncompressed model answering with a timeline (with a
one-line answer, Qwen3-VL-8B is well above it on ActivityNet and Charades-STA, \cref{sec:transfer}), which makes the floors a necessary
reference for every comparison that follows.
